Desktop IME · one bar, the whole loop

One bar. The whole loop.

Desktop AI work is a loop — bring context in, produce your output, refine it. Today it's scattered across apps, the clipboard, a translator, a chat box, and every hop drops context on the floor. This IME keeps the whole loop on one always-present bar, right where your cursor already is.

01 / Context

Input quality is no longer about typing speed

As AI becomes a daily collaborator, what matters is how fast and how faithfully you can hand it a complex intent.

On desktop, the core workflows are still office productivity and development. The design question is not just how to capture speech, but how to let people hand off a complex intent quickly enough to keep their work moving — especially in long-form writing and Vibe Coding.

02 / Problem

Raw transcription isn't usable text

Speech-to-text captures words literally. But real speech never arrives clean — and cleaning it up is work.

Raw voice transcription captures words literally, but real speech includes:

  • Self-corrections — “month over month, sorry, year over year.”
  • Fillers and false starts — “um,” “like,” “let me think.”
  • Topic drift — a lunch tangent in the middle of a business recap.

This creates heavy cleanup work before the text becomes usable.

The core tensionLiteral fidelity vs usable intent clarity
Exact wording

Quotes, numbers, commitments — you need it word-for-word.

Clean meaning

Drafting, summarizing, prompt writing — you need clear intent, not the mess.

Core view The same clip needs both readings depending on the task — a single output style is not enough.
03 / Design constraints

Make voice useful without taking people out of their work

The opportunity was not another destination AI app. It was a system layer that could help in the moment, without making speech feel less trustworthy.

Stay in the flow

Voice input has to remain close to the caret and the active task — not pull people into a separate AI workspace.

Keep the source recoverable

Quotes, numbers, and commitments matter. Any cleaner interpretation must keep the original words available.

Avoid a feature menu

One bar cannot become another complex command surface; it needs to surface the next useful action from context.

Work across real desktop tasks

The model must stretch from long-form writing to cross-language work and prompt editing — not solve a single demo case.

Design implication The system needs one stable entry point, context-aware actions, and a visible boundary between what was said and what AI resolved.
04 / Approach

One bar that turns context into the next action

Rather than another destination AI app, Voice IME stays close to the caret: one entry point, context-aware actions, and explicit control over transformation.

The system is built around three principles:

  • One entry point — every voice task starts from the same lightweight bar, rather than a scattered set of tools.
  • Context-aware actions — the selection, caret, clipboard, and active app determine what the bar can do next.
  • Control before automation — users can choose the right reading of speech and verify what changes before accepting it.
The control layerTwo modes, one clip — nothing lost
Verbatim Mode

Preserves wording fidelity for precision-sensitive tasks — quotes, numbers, commitments.

Intent Mode

Resolves spoken repairs, drops low-value noise, and outputs a cleaner structure for drafting.

Not a black box Users can compare states and recover the raw transcript at any time — Intent is a view, not a rewrite.
Verbatim annotationThree tiers, three treatments

Verbatim shows everything actually said — raw. The styling never rewrites; it only marks what each span becomes in Intent. Three semantic tiers, three visual treatments — never one blanket strikethrough.

06 / In action

See it in the work people already do

Each scenario follows the same arc: current context, a spoken request, an interpreted action, and a result the user can still check.

Long-form writing
From spoken thought to draft
A writer dictates a rough idea in Verbatim, then uses Intent to turn it into a sendable paragraph — with the original always available.
Cross-language work
Transform a selected passage
A user selects Chinese text and asks for natural English in a professional tone. The bar uses the selection as context and returns an editable preview.
Vibe coding
Make a request executable
A developer selects a loose requirement and says, “turn this into a clear task for my agent.” The result becomes a structured, inspectable prompt.

09 / Reflection

From character entry to intent transfer

The reframe
Voice input isn't character entry. It's intent transfer.
Why it matters

Reframing voice from character entry to intent transfer means faster capture, lower post-edit cost, and higher trust in the generated text — because you can always see and recover what was actually said.

End-to-end ownership

Driven from insight to execution: problem framing, interaction model, capability logic, and an implementation-aligned demo. Not only a UI concept — shipped as a working, demonstrable behavior layer.
