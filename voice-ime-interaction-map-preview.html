One shell, three ways in.

Voice IME begins from either an explicit voice entry or the text already on screen. The shell stays familiar; the trigger determines the flow.

How the system starts

Two ways to speak. One context-aware surface.

Voice input has two distinct entry points. A selected passage opens a third, context-aware surface for spoken instructions and suggested actions.

VOICE INPUT

Same voice lifecycle; different entry and placement.
01 · Persistent entry · mouse click

Start voice from anywhere

A visible launcher makes voice discoverable. Click it when you want to speak before you have a text target.

Click & Speak↘
Ready→Listening→Resolve→Review result
02 · Cursor entry · hotkey

Speak directly into the cursor

A hotkey invokes the same bar at the active caret, so the result returns to the document already in progress.

⌘
Ready at caret→Listening→Resolve→Insert / review

CONTEXT-TRIGGERED INTELLIGENCE

Selection changes what the same shell can help with.
03 · Selection surface

Say what you want changed — or choose a suggestion.

After a user selects text, the bar can accept a spoken instruction or recommend actions based on the selection: Translate for foreign text; Formalize or Rewrite for a draft.

Please postpone the launch until the localization review is complete.
Selection ready→Speak / choose action→Apply→Preview · accept · edit · undo

State rule: Listening is a voice-input state. Translate, Formalize, and Rewrite are context actions — not parallel system states. Every transformation ends in a reviewable result.
